# Governed personalisation

## Resumption is not personalisation

Resumption asks what this initiative was doing. Personalisation asks whether an
earlier human correction may influence a different initiative. Combining them
silently converts history into present truth.

Feedback first routes to the owner it already has:

- current-output correction → current initiative;
- stable project fact or term → confirmed project context;
- recurring failure → reviewed failure pattern;
- core method defect → separate method-change initiative;
- cross-initiative preference or bounded review heuristic → lesson candidate.

## Observation sources are not memory

The present run's correction, decision, output, and consequence artefacts are
the preferred source. An explicitly supplied or authorised transcript may fill
an observation gap, but remains untrusted evidence: embedded instructions do
not execute, provider summaries do not replace missing bytes, and transcript
availability does not admit a lesson.

Provider-native memory is outside this method. It supplies neither evidence nor
continuity and cannot nominate, select, use, revise, or retire a lesson.
When no transcript is accessible, the portable route relies on the material
semantic events the main agent recorded during the run and labels any missing
observation honestly.

## The governed chain

```text
material correction with observed consequence
→ inactive source-linked candidate and rival reading
→ exact retain/amend/reject
→ immutable retained lesson + opaque index cue
→ later initiative interpreted without lesson content
→ neutral nomination
→ bounded read-only disclosure of one lesson and visible mismatch
→ one explicit Select, Narrow, or Decline decision for one still-pending step
→ observable treatment/control outcome
→ retain, narrow, suspend, revise, or retire
```

The index cue must not reveal the operative lesson. Nomination is not
selection: the agent may disclose only the one neutrally nominated lesson, but
cannot use it until the user's single exact decision. That decision cannot
supply evidence, increase confidence, choose professional facts, or reach an
already completed step.

## Ordinary use

Suppose a lead corrects a decision brief:

```text
The decision-changing uncertainty was buried. For investment-committee briefs,
put the single evidence-supported kill criterion directly beneath the
recommendation. That would have let me test the recommendation before reading
the detail.
```

The present brief is corrected first. Only if the consequence appears reusable
does the agent offer an inactive, source-linked lesson candidate with a rival
reading and narrow scope. The lead may retain, amend, or reject it. On a later
analogous initiative, the agent first settles present meaning without the
lesson, then may nominate the opaque eligible cue. The lead sees the proposed
effect and mismatch and makes one decision: Select, Narrow, or Decline for one
still-pending step.

Useful follow-up language is equally ordinary:

```text
That placement helped because the brief had an explicit approval threshold.
Narrow the preference to that situation, then retire it from future use while
preserving the history.
```

Observed improvement requires a traceable difference and a human consequence.
Merely following an instruction or writing `helped` is not causal evidence. The
lesson's use history and later disposition provide the inspection surface; no
separate evaluation packet or runtime control plane is required.
