# Domain charter

The domain charter records what the installed method serves and protects before initiative-specific work begins.

## Consultation topics

- professional users, decision owners, and review responsibilities;
- recurring commissions and judgeable outcomes;
- consequential native artefacts and candidate forms;
- source classes, systems of record, and admissible uses;
- domain vocabulary and meanings that commonly drift;
- authority scopes, intended uses, and promotion seams;
- materiality, source-conflict, and challenge triggers;
- model, tool, confidentiality, and access constraints;
- recovery and re-derivation expectations;
- explicit non-scope and the first real acceptance episode.

## Method

Survey existing repository evidence and offer candidate answers for correction. Explore one topic until settled, then move on. Ask only where different answers materially alter recipes, agents, source custody, protected targets, or human authority. Record assumptions separately from confirmed professional facts.

Before writing, present settled, inferred, omitted, and unresolved topics and obtain exact human confirmation. The resulting charter should be concise enough to guide the method, not a speculative domain encyclopedia.

## Revision

Refine sections in place through superseding revisions and retained decisions. A new domain requires a separately observed profile; it does not inherit another profile's fitness by analogy.

## Handoff

Entry and orchestration read the charter as standing project context. Initiative meanings and work orders remain separate and may narrow but not silently contradict it.
