# Discuss the work without asking the client to adopt our vocabulary

These questions help tailor the supporting method to an analyst's actual work; they do not assume that semantic comparability is the client's only problem. Preserve useful automation and professional judgment already in service. Do not promise that more agents, a mapping ledger, or an external data provider will improve it.

## Start with an episode, not a diagnosis

Ask: “Show me the last model update that made you stop and check it. What had you asked for, and what did you notice first?”

Listen for a source-selection failure, a misread figure, invented reporting structure, a formula edit, a stale assumption, or an output that was correct but awkward to use. They imply different interventions. Ask to see the original request, relevant source region, and model before and after, not only the repaired answer.

Do not lead with “Was this a semantic admission failure?” Translate only after the practitioner describes the work.

## Recover the intended professional job

Ask: “In this section, did you want the company’s numbers reproduced, your own adjusted analysis, or a forecast?”

A demand for faithful reported history implies preservation of reporting detail and source basis. A demand for adjusted analysis permits an explicit transformation. A forecast permits a number that nobody has reported, but it needs assumptions and a declared role. “Make the model consistent” does not settle which job was intended.

Follow with: “Show me a change you would accept in one of those sections but reject in another.” That example is more informative than general agreement with our categories.

## Find the source rule and the reason behind it

Ask: “Which source should control this particular line, and when is the agent allowed to use another one?”

“Use the filing” may mean provenance discipline, detailed row coverage, a chosen vintage, or a rule specific to historical actuals. It does not establish that management guidance and internal forecasts must also come from filings. Ask: “When two company documents differ, what do you need to know before choosing?”

The resulting instruction should preserve the actual source restriction, not a generic hierarchy we invented.

## Test the template’s flexibility

Ask: “What about this template is fixed, and what do you expect the agent to adapt to the company?”

Listen separately for layout, formulas, scenario controls, reporting line detail, and analytical definitions. A client may want formatting preserved but company-specific rows added. Another may require a stable normalised structure with an explicit bridge. Ask: “Can you show me one acceptable row change and one that changes what the model means?”

Do not treat every structural change as damage or every generic template row as financial authority.

## Understand omissions and carry-forwards

Ask: “When a new disclosure does not let us update a line safely, what should happen to the old assumption and the conclusion that depends on it?”

Possibilities include an explicitly retained base case, an alternative scenario, withholding a particular claim, or stopping the update. “Leave it alone” is incomplete until the status of the dependent output is clear. Ask: “What would make you wrongly believe this part had been updated?”

This determines how the candidate records an unresolved update, rather than simply adding a caveat at the end.

## Locate the review burden

Ask: “After the agent produces the workbook, where does your time go: finding sources, checking meanings, checking formulas, understanding what changed, or deciding whether the assumptions are sensible?”

Do not count faster production alone as a gain. Ask the practitioner to walk through an actual check. Then ask: “Which of these could you stop redoing if the agent left the right evidence, and which would you still insist on judging yourself?”

A brief exception list is useful only if the underlying routine work is inspectable. It is not permission to hide untested regions.

## Elicit a valid difficult case, not only a failure

Ask: “Show me a number that looks inconsistent with the source at first glance but is legitimate in your model.”

This may reveal a unit conversion, deliberate adjustment, forecast, scenario, or company-specific convention. Such cases protect against a skill that appears safe because it refuses useful work. Ask: “What must remain visible so another analyst does not mistake that for a reported fact?”

## Check the real environment and boundaries

Ask: “Where can the agent read the exact source and model, where may it create a copy, and is it allowed to send those materials to another agent or provider?”

Use demonstrated client access, not the provider brand. Ask which missing operation would justify a setup task and which should instead result in an analyst-readable proposed change. Do not request credentials or imply that licensing and organisation policy can be bypassed.

## Read the meaning back

Use a concrete paraphrase: “For historical actuals, you want the filing’s reported detail retained. Changes to your forecast can use your assumptions, but they must remain labelled as assumptions. If a definition changes, this particular conclusion must stay conditional until you decide. Is that an accurate account, or have I joined different rules?”

Record the answer as a proposed local instruction with scope and examples. Agreement to a paraphrase is not evidence that an agent will follow it reliably.

## Illustrative concierge exchange — not a transcript quotation

**Analyst:** “Update the model for these results. Keep our base-case forecast assumptions.”

**Concierge:** “I’ll update reported actuals from the supplied filing and preserve the forecast assumptions. I’ll adapt reported rows only where your template permits it. If a changed definition affects the forecast, I’ll show that separately rather than decide it for you.”

**Later, after the work and declared checks:** “The historical updates are in the candidate copy. I kept the detailed working-capital rows instead of blending them with the adjusted release. Organic growth remains unresolved because the acquisition contribution is not disclosed; the reported-growth comparison is available, but it cannot support an organic-growth conclusion. Your forecast assumptions are unchanged. The attached change record shows what was checked. The one decision needed is whether to keep the current forecast under that limitation or ask for an alternative scenario.”

The actual message must match observed work. The example is not a script for claiming checks that were never performed.
