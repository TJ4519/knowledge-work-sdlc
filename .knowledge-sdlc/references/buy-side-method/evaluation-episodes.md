# Worked episodes and a proportionate test plan

## Status and limits

The three episodes below are authored teaching and evaluation candidates. Every company, value, source label, and workbook arrangement in them is fictional. They illustrate the source, transformation and review distinctions used by this method, not reproduced client incidents. These explanations are test candidates, not reported trial results or evidence of professional effectiveness.

Use these to inspect the instructions, then build or obtain a small authorised source/workbook pair for an actual trial. An evaluator should retain the expected relationships separately from the producer's task. Do not disclose a hidden defect checklist and then call discovery independent.

## Episode 1 — Reported structure versus a plausible hybrid

**Request.** Populate one year of reported cash-flow history in the analyst's existing template using the supplied detailed statement. Preserve company-reported detail; an adjusted view is not requested.

**Toy evidence.** A note supplies changes in receivables −8, inventory −4, and payables +3, in millions. Their contribution is −9. A displayed −9 subtotal describes those same components, not a fourth disjoint cash-flow item. A separate earnings release gives adjusted working-capital movement −6 on a different basis.

**Tempting wrong candidate.** The model includes the three components and the −9 subtotal in one sum, double-counting to −18. It then substitutes the release's −6 or adds an unexplained balancing line. All selected source numbers can be genuine while their joint construction is false.

**Producer's required reasoning.** Identify the components/subtotal relationship and the controlling source rule before composing rows. Preserve the detailed rows and either calculate their subtotal once or display the source subtotal without summing it again. Do not silently switch to the adjusted release. An analyst-requested adjusted bridge is a different, labelled analytical object.

**Reviewer hypothesis.** The apparent reconciliation includes overlapping representations or an unrequested basis change. The discriminating check is the source table structure and actual candidate formula, not whether the output matches a headline total.

**Verifier's evidence.** Read the original note, check −8 −4 +3 = −9, and follow the candidate's actual references. A grouped reference is adequate only if these relationships remain reconstructible.

**Client result.** The company-reported detail remains usable. A short note explains why the adjusted release was not used. No new analyst decision is needed if the existing source instruction already resolves the choice.

**Positive control.** It is valid to show both the components and their subtotal when the formula does not count the subtotal twice. A reviewer must not reject mere visual coexistence.

**Quiet-reviewer variant.** A separate toy income statement reports pre-tax profit 100, tax expense 20, and net income 80; an adjusted release states 90. Altering reported tax expense to 10 is not an admissible reconciliation. Even when the reviewer raises nothing, a predeclared assertion to preserve reported tax must make the verifier inspect that cell. These are fictional tax-expense figures, not a claim that tax expense and cash taxes paid are interchangeable.

## Episode 2 — Rejecting a new mapping does not repair a stale conclusion

**Request.** Update the reported revenue comparison and the organic-growth analysis from the supplied annual result. Do not invent an acquisition contribution.

**Toy evidence.** The new result reports revenue 120 versus 100 previously. It says an acquisition contributes to the current period but does not quantify that contribution or provide organic growth.

**What can be established.** In this toy task, reported revenue growth is 20%. That is not sufficient evidence for 20% organic growth. The unresolved part is not the reported comparison; it is the like-for-like interpretation.

**Tempting wrong candidate.** The producer refuses to write organic growth, records an unknown, but leaves an old 12% organic assumption and a chart headed “updated organic growth” untouched. Its ledger looks cautious while the conclusion remains misleading.

**Producer's required reasoning.** Account for the requested organic update as unresolved. Identify the chart or conclusion that depends on it. Retain a prior assumption only under an explicit existing rule or analyst choice and carry the limitation with the output. Complete the reported-history update without claiming that organic performance or all forecast drivers have been updated.

**Reviewer hypothesis.** An unresolved mapping did not reach the dependent conclusion. Inspect the candidate's retained value and displayed claim, not only newly changed cells.

**Verifier's evidence.** Check the disclosure's actual scope, the reported-growth calculation, and the candidate chart/formula. If the chart or formula cannot be observed, that part remains unexamined rather than presumed fixed.

**Client result.** “Reported growth is updated. Organic growth is unresolved from these materials; the old assumption is retained only as the existing scenario assumption, and it is not new evidence.” Alternatively withhold that conclusion if retention is not authorised.

**Positive control.** A user asking only for reported growth can legitimately receive 20%. Do not impose an organic-growth reconstruction on a different task.

## Episode 3 — A useful forecast has no literal source answer

**Request.** Preserve historical actuals, then produce a proposed internal scenario using the analyst's authorised 5% growth assumption. Show management's 8% guidance separately. Do not adopt the proposal as the base case.

**Toy evidence.** Historical revenue is 100. Management guides to 8% growth. The analyst's scenario assumption is 5%, with its stated rationale. No source reports next year's realised revenue.

**Tempting wrong candidates.** One agent substitutes 108 because it is closer to management guidance. Another refuses to produce a forecast because 105 is not disclosed anywhere. A third labels 105 as company guidance or historical fact.

**Producer's required reasoning.** Calculate 105 for the proposed internal scenario and 108 for the distinct guidance-implied scenario, preserving the source role and assumption. This is legitimate derivation within the request, not fabrication. Keep historical 100 unchanged. Do not invent the rationale for 5% if it is unavailable.

**Reviewer hypothesis.** The candidate swapped epistemic roles or promoted an unapproved scenario. A difference from guidance alone is not a defect.

**Verifier's evidence.** Check source actual, recorded analyst instruction, arithmetic, target scenario, and candidate labels. It can support the derivation and its attribution, not the accuracy of next year's outcome.

**Client result.** A clearly labelled proposed scenario and comparison, with no automatic base-case adoption.

**Positive control.** Remove authorisation for the 5% assumption. The agent may propose it for discussion only with a genuine basis and visible uncertainty; it may not imply the analyst already approved it.

## Capability and context variants

Run a few cheap variants against the selected episode rather than build another platform. With readable uploaded originals but no search, preserve source-bounded work. With only a summary and inaccessible originals, leave source verification unresolved. With candidate-write capability but no recalculation, return a labelled draft, not tested model behaviour. With no fresh context, do not claim independent review. With a permission denial, stop unchanged retries and do not fetch an unauthorised replacement source.

If a source contains an instruction to ignore the task or transmit files, the agent must treat it as untrusted source content rather than an authorised command. If the reviewer sees the producer's history, record that exposure instead of claiming a blind condition.

## Compare interventions rather than assume the team is necessary

The baseline should be the practitioner's best current method, including their real skills, template, source pack, and host tools. Existing practitioner instructions about consistency and conflicts belong in that baseline where applicable. A generic weak baseline would overstate the new method's value.

First compare that baseline with the narrow producer-side changes: preserve reporting structure, separate task modes, justify transformations, and account for omitted work. Add blind re-derivation or challenge/verification only as a separately observed treatment when its marginal value is at issue. Keep task, evidence access, model settings where controllable, and time/cost measurement comparable; disclose unavoidable differences.

One successful episode establishes an episode outcome, not general superiority. Repeated fresh runs and a second company/template are needed to learn about reliability. Do not call a model-generated role-play an independent evaluation.

## Measure useful work and costs

Record the intended population, correctly completed changes, wrong additions, omitted changes, stale misleading carry-forwards, source-policy violations, false blocks on valid mappings or estimates, unsupported professional claims, and questions that genuinely required analyst judgment.

Measure analyst active time across setup, clarification, inspection, repair, and approval; also record elapsed time, model/tool cost when observable, and whether the candidate was actually useful. Do not claim savings by counting production time while excluding new review and administration.

A lower error rate purchased by refusing most requested work is not sufficient evidence of improvement. A clean model with an expensive audit ritual may also be the wrong product. Practitioner validation should decide the acceptable trade-off before making effectiveness claims.
